# Mission 00.1 — What Are NOAI and IOAI, and Why Are We Taking This Course?

**Duration:** 75 minutes, plus a required 120-minute diagnostic completed before class or in a separate supervised block

**Essential question:** What will these competitions actually ask us to know and do, and how will this course prepare us?

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, students can:

1. explain what NOAI and IOAI are and how the Chinese national pathway connects to the international competition;
2. compare NOAI Round 1, NOAI Round 2, and the main IOAI competition stages;
3. distinguish theory and reasoning, practical implementation, open-ended problem solving, and teamwork;
4. explain the purpose and sequence of this course in their own words;
5. use the Input → Processing → Output lens to inspect a real competition task without jumping immediately to a model;
6. identify their assigned learning route and the evidence that will demonstrate progress;
7. interpret the diagnostic as baseline evidence by naming one current strength and two priority gaps.

## Annual Rule Boundary

Competition rules, dates, eligibility, platforms, permitted tools, and task formats can change every year. The 2026 information below is a dated reference snapshot, not a permanent rulebook. The teacher must display the access date for the current official documents. Current organiser documents and later clarifications always override this lesson.

## 2. Talk Robin 1 — Knowing Is Not Yet Doing

Discuss both prompts:

1. A student can define a convolutional neural network but cannot train or evaluate one. Which competition demands could the student meet, and which could the student not meet?
2. A student can run a copied notebook but cannot explain its metric, errors, or limitations. Is that evidence of competition readiness? Why or why not?

Each student must state one difference between **knowing about AI** and **using AI to solve a task**.

## 3. Entry Check — Commit Before the Reveal

Mark each claim as agree or disagree and write one reason. Keep the original response so that it can be corrected at the end.

1. NOAI and IOAI are two names for the same event.
2. NOAI Round 1 and Round 2 test essentially the same abilities in the same format.
3. IOAI is mainly a contest about selecting the most advanced available model.
4. Finishing videos and copying working notebooks would fulfil the purpose of this course.
5. Competition rules and permitted tools must be checked again every year.

## 4. Core Pattern — The Competition Map

### 4.1 How NOAI and IOAI Connect

For the 2026 reference pathway, NOAI is the Chinese national competition and selection pathway; IOAI is the international competition for national teams. Selection procedures are controlled by the organisers and must be rechecked each year.

| Stage | 2026 reference format | Main demand | What successful evidence looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| NOAI Round 1 — Level Test | Individual, paper-based, 120 minutes | Python, computer fundamentals, AI concepts, algorithms, calculations, and code reasoning | Correct answers plus explanations, traces, and calculations |
| NOAI Round 2 — China Stage | Applied, computer-based team challenge | Use Python and data/AI libraries to solve a practical problem | A valid, explainable, reproducible solution under the stated rules |
| National selection | Organiser-controlled selection process | Strong performance plus compliance with current selection requirements | Official qualification evidence |
| IOAI At-Home Round | Three educational tasks released before the event; not part of the final individual score in 2026 | Learn the task families, platform, data, baselines, and evaluation process | Working experiments and an explanation of what was learned |
| IOAI Individual Contest 1 | Three tasks related to the at-home task families; six hours in 2026 | Transfer prior learning under time and environment constraints | Valid submissions, measured improvements, and rule compliance |
| IOAI Individual Contest 2 | Three new tasks; six hours in 2026 | Rapid task recognition, creativity, implementation, and debugging | A useful solution built from an unfamiliar statement |
| IOAI Team Challenge | Separate collaborative challenge; the 2026 task involved AI for humanoid robots | Teamwork, integration, communication, and practical problem solving | A functioning team solution and defensible decisions |

The important progression is:

| First | Then | Finally |
|---|---|---|
| understand and reason about Python and AI | build and evaluate complete AI workflows | solve unfamiliar tasks under time, compute, tool, and submission constraints |

### 4.2 What Competition Content Actually Includes

The 2026 NOAI syllabus separates the content by competition demand:

| NOAI syllabus section | Main content | Primary preparation target |
|---|---|---|
| A — General Computer Skills | Python, control flow, data structures, functions, modules, sorting, and searching | Round 1 |
| B — AI Foundations | AI concepts, learning paradigms, statistics, classical ML, neural networks, evaluation, and ethics | Round 1 |
| C — Advanced Computer Skills | NumPy, Pandas, visualisation, data cleaning, and feature engineering | Round 2 |
| D — Advanced AI Skills | PyTorch, training loops, computer vision, NLP, audio, multimodality, and model use under constraints | Round 2 |

The IOAI syllabus labels topics as **Theory**, **Practice**, or **Both**:

- **Theory:** explain how and why a method works;
- **Practice:** know what it does, when to use it, how to implement it, and how to interpret the output;
- **Both:** connect the mathematical or conceptual idea to a working solution.

Real IOAI tasks are not merely “train a standard classifier.” The official 2026 tasks included:

| Released task | Data or setting | Core challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Find the Order | Speech and dialogue turns | Reconstruct the original order of shuffled speaker turns |
| Robot Chasing | Natural language and gridworld behaviour | Predict which robot behaviour an instruction describes |
| Potato | Word meaning and pairwise semantic comparisons | Find a hidden word within a limited number of turns |
| Double Agent Dilemma | Images, ResNet, and Vision Transformer behaviour | Use systematic disagreement between two accurate architectures |
| Ghost of the Machine | Text passages | Locate machine-altered parts of an archive |
| IOAI Field | Spatial functions and generation | Train a generator from scratch under explicit model constraints |

These examples show why preparation must include task reading, data, mathematics, models, metrics, debugging, error analysis, and time management.

### 4.3 What These Competitions Are Not

They are not:

- a contest in who can use a chatbot most quickly;
- a vocabulary test only;
- a programming contest only;
- a guarantee that the largest or newest model will win;
- an environment with unlimited internet, compute, packages, or pretrained models;
- evidence that copied code has been understood.

### 4.4 The First Reusable Lens

Every task in this course begins with:

| Lens | Question |
|---|---|
| Input | What data, question, resources, or observations are given? |
| Processing | What pattern must be recognised, what method or template fits, and what details must be added and verified? |
| Output | What exact answer, prediction, file, model, explanation, or action must be produced? |
| Metric | How will quality be measured? |
| Constraints | What time, compute, tools, data, and submission rules apply? |
| Evidence | What can the student explain, reproduce, test, and defend? |

The technical modelling workflow comes later. Session 1 establishes why students need it.

## Session 1 Gate

A student passes the Session 1 gate only when the student can, without copying the tables:

- distinguish NOAI from IOAI;
- compare the main national and international stages;
- name at least three different capability types the competitions require;
- explain the purpose and sequence of this course;
- interpret the diagnostic as baseline evidence and name one strength and two priority gaps;
- analyse one task using Input → Processing → Output;
- identify a current rule that must be verified rather than assumed.

This lesson does not award competition readiness, selection readiness, or model-workflow mastery. It establishes the destination and the reason for the learning sequence. Formal task-recognition and baseline training begins only after the required Python, data, and ML foundations are in place.

## Teacher Debrief

After class, record:

- which competition distinction students still confuse;
- whether students understand that theory and practice are both required;
- which students cannot yet explain the course purpose;
- which current-rule questions need an organiser source;
- which question-level patterns reveal missing Python, algorithm, mathematics, ML/DL, evaluation, or ethics foundations;
- whether the assigned route is appropriate or requires additional diagnostic evidence.

Do not spend most of the lesson on dates, registration, awards, or promotional claims. The instructional target is the competition structure, capability demands, course purpose, and evidence standard.
