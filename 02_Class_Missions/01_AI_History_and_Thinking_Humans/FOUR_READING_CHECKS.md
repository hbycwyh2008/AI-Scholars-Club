# Four Reading Checks: *AI: A Guide for Thinking Humans*

Students have spent approximately one month reading the book in detail. These four 70-minute checks revisit Chapters 1, 2, 6, 7, and 8. Use the chapter questions and examples in the class handout. The NOAI B-topic appendix is outside these four checks.

| Check | Handout sections and chapter questions | Evidence to explain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Chapters 1 and 2 | Four AI approaches; neural-network training diagram; Chapter 1 and 2 questions | Compare GPS and the perceptron. Explain how loss, backpropagation, gradient descent, and learning rate adjust weights and biases. |
| 2 — Chapter 6 | Chapter 6 question; visual-recognition and long-tail examples in the milestone table | Distinguish test-set accuracy, generalization, and real-world robustness. Use road salt, sandbags, shortcuts, or adversarial examples. |
| 3 — Chapter 7 | Trustworthy AI section; Chapter 7 question; ACLU facial-recognition case | Analyse group bias, interpretability, privacy, human review, and accountability. Propose practical responses. |
| 4 — Chapter 8 and extension | Rosie Q-learning section; Chapter 8 question; AlphaGo extension | Explain delayed reward, exploration and exploitation; compare a simplified robot task with a game-specific system. |

## Each 70-minute check

1. **10 minutes:** Revisit the relevant handout sections and locate evidence in the book.
2. **25 minutes:** Independently answer the corresponding student question.
3. **20 minutes:** Explain the answer and challenge one another: what does the example show, and what does it not show?
4. **15 minutes:** Revise the answer using discussion and teacher feedback.

The milestone table is a reference for selecting examples, not a list of dates to memorize. The teacher answer key remains in the teacher copy and is not a student memorization task.
